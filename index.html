2 · The rhythm

Most of us think the big appointment is the important one.

The colour day. The change. It does matter. But the result lives in the in-between, and those are the weeks nobody books.

The toner at week four that stops the colour drifting. The treatment that protects what was just created. The blow-dry that makes Monday morning feel manageable. Small appointments, booked on purpose rather than when something has already gone wrong.

The environment here moves your colour. Your plan holds it.

Heat, humidity and the water all work on your hair between visits, every day, whether or not anybody has planned for it. That is what the rhythm is for, and it is what your stylist writes down for you.

If your colour is blonde

Your blonde was exactly right when you left the salon. What happened?

Nothing went wrong. But something was missed. The toner appointment at week four is what holds your colour in place between visits.

Without it, warmth creeps back in, brightness fades, and by week six you are already counting down to your next colour appointment. Most women accept that as normal. It is not normal, it is only unplanned.

With it, week eight looks like week one.

Week 1Your colour appointment, the one you have always booked
Week 4 to 5A Tone Reset with your junior stylist, before the warmth returns
Week 8A blow-dry or a treatment, so the condition holds
Week 10Colour again, starting from hair in far better shape

Questions

The honest answers.

Is a junior stylist experienced enough?›
She is a specialist in the appointments she does most. Our junior stylists do more blow-dries, more toner appointments and more maintenance treatments than anyone else in the salon, and that repetition is what makes them brilliant at them. Every one of them is trained the Tara Rose way and signed off by a named senior on everything she offers you.
What is a Tone Reset?›
Toner and a blow-dry, thirty to forty-five minutes. It resets your tone before the warmth creeps back in, which is why week eight can still look like week one. For blonde and colour clients it is the appointment that makes the most visible difference for the least time in the chair.
How often should I be coming in?›
It depends on your hair and your colour, and your stylist writes yours down for you. A common rhythm is colour, then a Tone Reset at week four or five, then a blow-dry or a treatment around week eight, then colour again around week ten.
Does a course really work better than one treatment?›
One appointment improves things. A course changes them, because the repair builds session by session rather than starting again each time. Your stylist tracks it at every visit and will tell you honestly if a single session is all you need.

All four: Khalifa City A and Mamsha Al Saadiyat in Abu Dhabi, and Al Quoz 1 and Motor City in Dubai. A salon appears here only if it can actually deliver the appointment.
